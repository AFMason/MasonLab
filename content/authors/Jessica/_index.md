Jessica is an Honours Student building a low-cost, field-deployable diagnostic platform using digital droplet LAMP (ddLAMP). The technique compartmentalises isothermal amplification reactions into thousands of individual droplets, allowing precise counting and quantification of genetic targets without expensive lab equipment — well suited to point-of-care and resource-limited settings. A major part of the project is troubleshooting the platform's real-world reliability, from maintaining droplet stability at high temperatures to minimising false positives caused by primers binding to themselves rather than the target DNA.

They were drawn to ddLAMP by its position at the intersection of molecular biology and the physics of droplets, fluid interfaces, and thermodynamics — extending their natural interests in biology and physics while staying grounded in a practical problem, with plenty of room for the hands-on troubleshooting and iteration they enjoy.

Outside the lab, Jessica enjoys painting, reading, and learning about new discoveries in other fields.
