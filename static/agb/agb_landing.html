Do you work with retinoic acid?

We are building cell-sized beads that place retinoic acid where you want it, so your cells see a gradient rather than a bath. Early-stage, non-clinical, and open to collaborators.

Join the list

The problem, briefly

Retinoic acid goes into culture as a uniform bath and gets treated as a fixed dose. It is not fixed: it isomerises under ambient light, oxidises, and adsorbs to plastic and protein, so the delivered dose differs from the nominal one. And a bath has no position, so every cell sees the same input and protocols converge on a narrow band of regional identity.

In development the same molecule carries position as a spatial gradient. We are putting the source back in the dish: small beads, placed where you choose, releasing locally.

Where this is up to

Honest version. We can place beads at defined positions in standard plates, and we can show tunable, sustained release of a model cargo. Quantitative release of retinoic acid is still being measured, and we draw no conclusions yet on release kinetics or dose response.

If you run a retinoic-acid-dependent protocol and want to know what your cells actually see, or want to try a placed source instead of a bath, that is exactly who we are looking for. The biology comes from you; the delivery and the measurement come from us.
