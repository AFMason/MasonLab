Model description

The model solves one-dimensional radial reaction-diffusion for one spherical bead at the centre of a spherical volume of medium. The total volume is the medium volume plus the bead volume. The outer boundary has no flux. The solver uses a finite-volume grid (40 cells in the bead, 140 geometrically stretched cells outside) and an implicit (backward Euler) time step.

  • Two phases in the bead. Cargo is in a reservoir phase (microparticles or coacervate droplets, volume fraction φ) or in the continuous gel phase. Initial burst fraction goes into the gel phase at t = 0.
  • Reservoir exchange. dcr/dt = −k (cr − K·cfree), solved exactly for each cell and step. Small molecule model: k = 15 Dp / a² (linear driving force approximation for a sphere of radius a). Protein model: k = ln 2 / tesc, which lumps interfacial resistance and binding off-rate. tesc is the escape half-time under sink conditions.
  • Reservoir loss. Small molecule model: logistic erosion of the microparticles, φ(t) = φ0 S(t). Protein model: polycation chain scission, L(t)/L0 = exp(−ks t) with ks = ln 2 / t½ + s·E (spontaneous plus protease-driven). A droplet dissolves when L/L0 falls below its critical fraction. Critical fractions are log-normal (median ℓm, width σ), so S(t) = Φ((−kst − ln ℓm)/σ) / S(0). Cargo in lost reservoir volume moves to the gel phase.
  • Transport. Free diffusivity from Stokes–Einstein with water viscosity at the set temperature. Gel diffusivity from the Amsden obstruction-scaling model, Dg/D0 = exp(−π((rs+rf)/(ξ+2rf))²), with a Maxwell correction (1−φ)/(1+φ/2) for impermeable reservoir inclusions.
  • Serum albumin binding (small molecule model). Fast equilibrium with free fraction f. Effective diffusivity f·Dfree + (1−f)·Dcomplex (complex radius 3.5 nm). Reservoir partition acts on the free concentration. All plotted concentrations are free (bioactive) concentrations.
  • Sinks. First-order loss from the cargo half-life in all free phases, lumped cellular uptake in the medium only, and medium exchange events.

Limitations

  • The plate floor and the cell layer are not resolved. A bead that rests on a reflecting floor increases the local concentration near the contact point (up to approximately 2× very near the bead). This model does not include that effect.
  • One bead per well. No convection. Gel to medium partition is 1. Cellular uptake is a volumetric sink, not a surface sink at the cell layer.
  • Default values are illustrative. They are not measured values for any specific material. Use measured release data to set the reservoir parameters before you use the output quantitatively.
