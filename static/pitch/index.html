The idea in one minute

Every laboratory that grows human stem-cell models adds the same few signals, but no one measures what actually reaches the cells, so the same protocol gives different results in different laboratories. This Future Fellowship builds synthetic cells that release those signals by design and measure them in place, so a protocol becomes a specification that works the same way everywhere.

Why this, why now, why UOW

Why this matters

  • Reproducibility. Same stem-cell lines, one protocol, five laboratories: the laboratory was the largest source of variation (Volpato et al. 2018).
  • Replacing animals. Species differences limit what animal results tell us about people, and the 3Rs ask research to replace and reduce animal use.
  • National priority. The proposed national strategy names a platform for non-animal technologies (Action 28).

Why now

  • Last round. FT27 is the final Future Fellowship round; the ARC moves to project schemes from 2027.
  • New schemes open. ARC Explore opens in 2027, then Breakthrough; NHMRC Emerging Leadership follows.
  • Others are investing. The Dutch government put €124.5M into Ombion; Roche's Institute of Human Biology has up to 250 researchers.
  • The groundwork is paid. The DECRA built the lab; outputs and trained people are landing now.

Why UOW, and where it leads

  • In place already. Molecular Horizons, animal-free stem-cell labs, automated fabrication (DIB-BOT) and single-molecule measurement.
  • Track record. In seven months, one research assistant at 0.8 FTE took an idea to new IP.
  • Enabling personalised medicine. When a protocol works the same way every time, a person's own stem cells can be turned reliably into the cells needed to test which treatment suits them. Reproducible protocols are the precondition.

What changes

The signal a cell receives goes from assumed to measured.

Today: nominal dosing

  1. Dose addedBMP, Shh, RA pipetted in as a uniform bath
  2. Black boxDiffusion, binding, uptake, feeding, geometry
  3. Field at the cellNever measured
  4. Cell fateVaries by laboratory, batch and line

With synthetic cell engineering

  1. Designed sourceA synthetic cell that releases signal by design
  2. Measured in situSingle-molecule methods, absolute concentration
  3. Field at the cell
    Known, not assumed
  4. Cell fatePurer neuronal and glial populations, every time
Closed loop: fabricate · measure · learn · refine (automated on DIB-BOT)
